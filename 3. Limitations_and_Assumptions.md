\# Limitations and Assumptions

\## Geospatial Disease Surveillance & Intervention Prioritization

\---

\### 1. Assumptions

The following assumptions underpin the analysis:

\- \*\*Malaria risk\*\* from the Malaria Atlas Project is a suitable proxy for relative disease transmission risk across the study area.

\- \*\*Child population (ages 0–9)\*\* is an appropriate demographic proxy for population vulnerability in the context of malaria.

\- \*\*Seasonal rainfall (March–May)\*\* and \*\*surface water occurrence\*\* together provide a reasonable indication of environmental suitability for malaria transmission.

\- \*\*Health facility density\*\* is a useful spatial proxy for health-system presence, even though it does not capture service quality or capacity.

\- The selected MCDA weights (Disease Risk 40%, Environmental Suitability 25%, Population Vulnerability 20%, Health System Pressure 15%) represent a defensible prioritisation logic for surveillance and intervention planning.

\- Areas where multiple high-scoring risk factors overlap should receive higher priority for enhanced surveillance and targeted intervention.

\- The six selected counties form a coherent and policy-relevant study region for a portfolio-scale demonstration.

\---

\### 2. Limitations

\#### Data Limitations

\- Population rasters (WorldPop) are modelled estimates, not census enumerations.

\- Malaria risk surfaces are modelled products and do not represent confirmed case data.

\- Surface water occurrence indicates the presence of water over time but does not directly identify mosquito breeding sites.

\- The Kenya Master Health Facility Registry may contain incomplete, outdated, or inaccurately located records.

\- Some border and highland areas had limited or missing malaria risk data, requiring gap-filling or exclusion of a small number of cells.

\#### Methodological Limitations

\- The Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis results are sensitive to the choice of weights. Different weightings can shift the location and extent of high-priority zones.

\- Facility density reflects geographic distribution only. It does not account for facility capacity, staffing levels, commodity availability, or service quality.

\- Population pressure is a relative indicator and should not be interpreted as a precise measure of healthcare accessibility or travel time.

\- Standardization to a 1–5 scale simplifies continuous variation and can compress meaningful differences within classes.

\- The analysis is cross-sectional and does not incorporate temporal trends or seasonality beyond the selected rainfall window.

\#### Interpretive Limitations

\- The resulting priority map is a \*\*decision-support model\*\*, not a clinical or epidemiological prediction model.

\- High priority does not automatically mean that an area is currently experiencing high disease burden; it indicates elevated relative risk based on the selected criteria.

\- Results should support — not replace — programme expertise, local knowledge, and routine surveillance data.

\- Edge effects may slightly underestimate facility density near the outer boundaries of the study area because facilities outside the six counties were not included.

\---

\### 3. Recommended Use of Results

The outputs of this project are intended to:

\- Support geographic prioritisation discussions

\- Highlight areas where multiple risk factors coincide

\- Inform targeting of surveillance strengthening and community-level interventions

\- Provide a transparent and reproducible analytical framework

They should be interpreted alongside operational data, local context, and expert judgement.
