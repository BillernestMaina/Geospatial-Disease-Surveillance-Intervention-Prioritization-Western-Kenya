\# Geospatial Disease Surveillance & Intervention Prioritization

\### Western Kenya Case Study

\*\*Portfolio Project #3\*\*

\*\*Primary Organizational Fit:\*\* Clinton Health Access Initiative (CHAI)

\*\*Secondary Fit:\*\* World Food Programme (WFP)

\---

\## Project Overview

This project develops a geospatial decision-support framework to identify geographic priorities for enhanced disease surveillance and intervention planning in western Kenya.

It integrates:

\- Malaria risk

\- Environmental suitability (rainfall + surface water)

\- Population vulnerability (child population)

\- Health system pressure (population relative to facility density)

The final output is a \*\*Disease Surveillance Priority Index (DSPI)\*\* that highlights areas where multiple risk factors overlap.

\---

\## Study Area

Six counties in western Kenya:

\- Busia

\- Bungoma

\- Kakamega

\- Siaya

\- Vihiga

\- Kisumu

\---

\## Key Results

\- Total population analysed: \*\*≈ 8.07 million\*\*

\- Population living in High + Very High priority areas: \*\*≈ 3.68 million (46%)\*\*

\- Highest priority county: \*\*Busia\*\*

\- Lowest priority county: \*\*Kisumu\*\*

\- Priority health facilities identified: \*\*380\*\*
