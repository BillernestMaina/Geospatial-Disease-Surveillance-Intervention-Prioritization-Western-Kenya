\# Project Explanation

\## Geospatial Disease Surveillance & Intervention Prioritization in Western Kenya

\*\*Portfolio Project #3\*\*

\*\*Primary Organizational Fit:\*\* Clinton Health Access Initiative (CHAI)

\*\*Secondary Fit:\*\* World Food Programme (WFP)

\---

\### 1. Project Purpose

Disease-control programmes cannot treat every location with the same intensity. Some communities experience environmental conditions that favour disease transmission, contain larger concentrations of vulnerable populations, or have relatively limited health-system presence.

This project was designed to answer a practical programme question:

\> \*\*Where should disease surveillance and intervention resources be prioritized based on the intersection of disease risk, environmental conditions, population vulnerability, and health-system context?\*\*

Rather than producing a simple malaria map, the project develops a full \*\*geospatial decision-support framework\*\* that integrates multiple risk dimensions into a single, transparent priority index.

\---

\### 2. Why This Project Matters

Many health and humanitarian organisations (including CHAI and WFP) increasingly rely on geospatial intelligence to guide resource allocation, targeting, and monitoring.

This project demonstrates the ability to:

\- Move from raw multi-source data to a usable decision-support product

\- Combine epidemiological, environmental, demographic, and health-system layers

\- Apply Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) in a transparent and defensible way

\- Produce outputs that are relevant to programme managers (priority zones + priority facilities)

It is particularly aligned with CHAI’s work on geospatial data for health-service planning and disease-surveillance strengthening, and with WFP’s interest in climate and vulnerability-informed programming.

\---

\### 3. Study Area

The analysis focuses on six counties in western Kenya that are relevant for malaria and broader health programming:

\- Busia

\- Bungoma

\- Kakamega

\- Siaya

\- Vihiga

\- Kisumu

These counties offer a useful combination of population variation, environmental diversity, and differing health-system density, while remaining computationally manageable for a portfolio-scale project.

\---

\### 4. Analytical Framework

The project is structured around five components:

1\. \*\*Disease Risk\*\* – Malaria parasite rate (Malaria Atlas Project)

2\. \*\*Environmental Suitability\*\* – Seasonal rainfall (CHIRPS) + surface water occurrence (JRC)

3\. \*\*Population Vulnerability\*\* – Concentration of children aged 0–9 years (WorldPop)

4\. \*\*Health System Context\*\* – Population pressure relative to facility density (KMHFR)

5\. \*\*Integrated Priority\*\* – Weighted Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis

The final model is expressed as:

\*\*DSPI = (0.40 × Malaria Risk) + (0.25 × Environmental Suitability) + (0.20 × Population Vulnerability) + (0.15 × Health System Pressure)\*\*

\---

\### 5. Key Technical Steps

\- Defined and standardized the study area (wards and counties)

\- Prepared and harmonized multi-source raster and vector datasets

\- Standardized all indicators to a common 1–5 scale

\- Calculated facility density using Kernel Density Estimation

\- Derived population-to-facility pressure

\- Applied weighted overlay to generate the Disease Surveillance Priority Index

\- Classified priority levels and extracted Very High priority zones

\- Identified health facilities located inside or near the highest-priority areas

\- Produced zonal statistics at both county and ward levels

\---

\### 6. Main Findings

\- Total population analysed: approximately \*\*8.07 million\*\*

\- Population living in High or Very High priority areas: approximately \*\*3.68 million (46%)\*\*

\- Highest priority county: \*\*Busia\*\*

\- Lowest priority county: \*\*Kisumu\*\* (largely due to stronger facility coverage)

\- Number of priority health facilities identified: \*\*380\*\*

The highest priority zones are concentrated in parts of Busia and Bungoma, with additional high-priority wards in Kakamega and Siaya.

\---

\### 8. Portfolio Positioning

This project is best presented as:

\> “A geospatial decision-support framework that integrates disease risk, environmental conditions, population vulnerability, and health-system distribution to identify geographic priorities for enhanced disease surveillance and intervention planning.”

This framing is stronger than describing it simply as a “malaria mapping project.” It positions the work closer to the type of analytical support valued by organisations such as CHAI, national malaria programmes, and humanitarian agencies working on targeted interventions.

\---

\### 9. Limitations (Important for Interviews)

\- Environmental suitability is a proxy and does not equal actual transmission.

\- Population layers are modelled estimates.

\- Facility density does not capture service quality, staffing, or commodity availability.

\- Results are sensitive to the chosen MCDA weights.

\- The model should ideally be validated against routine surveillance data where available.

These limitations are acknowledged transparently and form part of responsible geospatial practice.

\---

\### 10. Possible Extensions

If authorised surveillance or programme data were available, the framework could be extended into a more operational \*\*Geospatial Disease Surveillance Intelligence\*\* system by incorporating:

\- Confirmed case data

\- Temporal trends

\- Facility reporting performance

\- Emerging hotspot detection
